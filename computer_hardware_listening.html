Last week, I decided to open up my old desktop computer to see what was inside. The first thing I noticed was a large green board connecting everything together — this is called the (1). It is the most important part because every other component plugs directly into it.

Next to it, I found a small but powerful chip. This is the (2), often described as the "brain" of the computer, because it processes all the instructions and calculations.

Right next to that chip, there were a few sticks of memory. These are called (3), and they temporarily store data that the computer is actively using. Unlike storage, this type of memory is lost when the computer is turned off.

For long-term storage, my computer had two options: an older, mechanical (4), which uses spinning disks, and a newer, faster (5), which has no moving parts and uses flash memory instead.

I also noticed a separate card dedicated to processing images and video. This is the (6), which is especially important for gaming and video editing.

To keep everything from overheating, there was a small device spinning constantly — the (7). Without it, the CPU could overheat and stop working.

Powering all of this was a metal box called the (8). It converts electricity from the wall socket into the correct voltage for the computer's components.

Finally, to actually use the computer, I need output and input devices: the (9) to display the image, and the (10) and (11) to type and click.
